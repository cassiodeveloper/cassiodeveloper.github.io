# DevSecOps is not a checklist

The DevSecOps Podcast was created to discuss software security as it happens in the field: with context, disagreement, limitations and decisions that must continue to work after the code reaches production.

A tool is not a strategy. A scanner is not an AppSec program. Compliance does not replace engineering. These principles guide the podcast's conversations.

## About the show

The podcast explores Application Security and DevSecOps through technical discussions, interviews, professional experience and analysis of real cases.

Episodes are useful to both newcomers and experienced professionals. The objective is not to oversimplify every problem, but to make each subject accessible without removing the complexity that matters.

Recurring topics include:

- secure development culture;
- Security Champions;
- SAST, DAST and vulnerability management;
- threat modeling;
- CI/CD and automation;
- Product Security;
- cloud and Zero Trust;
- AppSec careers and education;
- artificial intelligence applied to security.

## Production is the truth

One of the show's central ideas is that controls must survive the real environment. A process can look excellent in a presentation and still fail when it meets deadlines, legacy systems, false positives, dependencies between teams and delivery pressure.

The conversations therefore focus on operational questions:

- Who is responsible for making the decision?
- What can a developer fix with the available evidence?
- When should a task be automated, and when does it require human review?
- How can risk be reduced without turning the pipeline into a permanent blocker?
- Does the control work in production, or only in the document?

## People and perspectives

The show is hosted by Cássio B. Pereira and includes security, development and market professionals. Marcos Santos and Ben-Hur Santos are part of the podcast team, alongside guests who bring experience from different companies and specialties.

That diversity matters because AppSec does not happen inside a single role. Software security depends on collaboration between the people who design, build, operate, test, prioritize and take responsibility for products.
